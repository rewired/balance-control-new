Influence, majority and control

Each tile has its own contest for control. Add up each player’s real markers on that tile and any virtual Influence that applies there. The player with the highest total controls the tile if they lead alone. A tie for the lead or all totals at zero leaves the tile uncontrolled.

Example. Red has 2 Influence, Blue has 1 and Green has 1. Red controls the tile. If Blue gains 1 Influence there, Red and Blue are tied and neither controls it. Control updates as soon as the markers or their bonuses change.

How Lobbyists help

Work out control of a Lobbyist using real markers on that Lobbyist. If one player controls it, they get +1 virtual Influence on each tile that shares an edge with it, except other Lobbyists and the Start Committee. Bonuses from multiple controlled Lobbyists add up. A Lobbyist with a tied lead or no markers gives no bonus.

Example. Red controls two Lobbyists next to the same Committee. Red has no real marker on the Committee and Blue has one. Red leads there by 2 to 1 and can use it to Formalize Influence once the timing and payment requirements are met.

What control lets you do

  • A standard Committee or a Grassroots tile must be under your control to use its action. The Start Committee has a separate action available without control.
  • The sole leader on a Resort receives its full production. A positive tie shares the output among the tied leaders.
  • The sole leader on a Hotspot gets the chance to place a marker when it is first surrounded.

Virtual Influence counts toward these leads. Only real markers count toward the marker limit and only real markers on the board count toward victory.
